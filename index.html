Most Likely To

The question every friend group already argues about — now it's official.

A prompt drops on the big screen — who's most likely to fake their own death, marry into royalty, end up running a cult — and everyone votes at once. The board lights up with who got picked and by how much, and the score climbs from there.

🌶️

Would You Rather

Every question gets a little worse than the last. On purpose.

Two options, one vote each round. Whichever side the room favors less becomes the new baseline, so the dares ratchet up ladder by ladder until someone's stuck picking the lesser evil of something genuinely unhinged.

🤥

Two Truths and a Room

Everyone's a liar tonight. Vote to catch whose lie is whose.

Before playing, everyone quietly writes one true fact and one convincing lie about themselves. Each round surfaces one player's pair, and the room votes on which is the lie — score for spotting it, or for fooling everyone who got it wrong.

⛰️

Hill to Die On

Pick a side. Then defend it, out loud, while everyone watches to see if you cave.

A hot take drops and everyone slides from disagree to agree along a line. The two people furthest apart get thirty seconds to argue their case live, then the room votes again to see who actually swayed anybody.

🏠

Hometown Trivia

How well do you actually know these people?

Everyone quietly writes a few true facts about themselves at the start. Each round, one gets read out with no name attached, and the room has to guess who it belongs to — the author scores every time they stump someone.

🎯

Confidently Wrong

Trivia with a wager attached to your ego.

Standard multiple-choice trivia, except before locking in your answer you also bet ×1, ×2, or ×3 on how sure you are. Nail it and the wager pays off; miss it and it costs you the same amount — swagger is a real risk here.

🧠

Group Brain

No buzzer. No first-right winner. Just vibes and arithmetic.

A numeric question drops on the big screen — how many bones in a hand, how tall is Everest — and everyone types their own guess. Points scale with how close you land, and the room's average gets revealed too, because the crowd is sometimes smarter than any one guesser.

🎨

Doodle Chain

Telephone, but everyone's terrible at drawing.

One phone draws a prompt, the next phone sees only that drawing and has to caption it, the next sees only the caption and draws that — on and on around the whole room. No scoring here; once every chain unravels on the big screen, the room just votes for its favorite.

✏️

Speed Sketch

The picture doesn't wait for the end — everyone watches it happen live.

One player gets a secret word and starts drawing against the clock, and every stroke streams straight to everyone else's phone as it happens. The rest of the room free-types guesses in real time — the faster you're right, the more it's worth, and the artist's score depends on how many people actually read it.

🤐

Taboo Chain

Describe it without saying the words you'd normally reach for first.

One phone gets a secret phrase plus three words it can't use, then races the clock typing clues while the room free-types guesses from what comes through. Slip and use a banned word and the clue gets blocked before anyone even sees it — no do-overs, just try again.
